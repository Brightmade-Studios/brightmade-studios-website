Orbit

A private to-do app that comes to you, so the day reaches you before you have to go looking for it.

Private app

Most to-do apps ask you to remember to check them. Orbit comes to you instead.

Orbit holds work, home, projects, the family budget, the nightly routine and the thoughts that are not tasks yet. Everyone gets their own reminders on their own phone, and the people you invite see only their slice.

First thing in the morning

The day arrives before you do

At six a message lands on your phone, written fresh that day and naming what is due. It comes through the messaging app you already use, so there is nothing new to open and nothing to remember to check. By the time you pick up Orbit you already know where the day stands, which is the part most lists get backwards.

Opening the app

Work and home, side by side

Two lists, each in its own colour, each with its own categories. On a phone they are tabs; on a laptop they sit as columns. The header reads "Today's focus" and counts what is left. Anything with a time on it wears that time, so a glance separates what is waiting from what is merely near. Nothing here asks you to sort it out first.

When a thought lands

Say it, keep moving

Hold the button and talk, or type it if you would rather. A sentence comes back as a task, dated, filed, with the person attached. You see what it understood and tap once to keep it, so nothing is saved behind your back. Hand it a photo and it reads that too.

Bigger than one task

Projects with milestones you can move

Some things do not fit on one line. A project carries its milestones, a deadline and a progress bar, and any milestone can be pushed into today's list when it is finally the day for it, then pulled back out when the day changes. The work stays in one place; only what is live reaches your list.

The household money

Bills, split by pay period

The budget opens behind a face scan. Bills sit under the pay period they belong to, the 15th or the 30th, so one period's money can never quietly cover the other's. Each account shows what is in it and what is left once that period's bills are counted, green while it holds.

Last thing at night

The moon fills as you finish

The night screen stays dark whatever colour the rest of the app is wearing. A moon sits at the top and fills as you work down tonight's list. Finish it and the moon is whole, the heading changes to "That's everything." and underneath it says "Sleep well." Nothing carried over, nothing left running overnight.

Who sees what

People see only their slice

You add people by email and give each a role. Household shares the budget. Guardian keeps their own until you approve otherwise. Assistant never gets near it, and can be narrowed to a single named category of your work and nothing else. Change your mind later and it takes one tap.

  • Private by invite, no public sign-up
  • A written summary every morning
  • Reminders at the exact minute
  • Speak it, and it files itself
  • Opens and saves with no signal
  • Five palettes, light and dark
  • Backed up every four hours

We built Orbit from nothing for one household, and it has grown with them rather than around a roadmap. Nothing goes live by hand; every change ships through a review, with the app, its rules and its database moving together.
