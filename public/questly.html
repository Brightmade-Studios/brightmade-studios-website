Saying it is done

A tap asks. A parent pays.

Your child taps a finished job and the stars do not move. The job turns to show it is with both of you, their character stands there thinking, and both your phones buzz. Nothing a child does in Questly pays out on its own. The stars always come from a parent.

On a parent's phone

Three answers, never silence

You see what is waiting and how long it has waited, with the standard for that job on the card: what done looks like, agreed in advance. Approve, and the stars land. Send it back with one of five short reasons, and it returns to today's list as "not yet". Or say no for today, with a reason. All three go in the family's record.

What the stars are for

A shop to walk around

Real rewards, with prices you set and photos of the real thing if you like. Your child fills a basket and watches the total move against what is left, so what is affordable gets found out by trying. Nothing out of reach is hidden. It can become the thing being saved for instead, which is a decision your child makes rather than one that happens to them.

At the checkout

Where the stars came from

Before your child asks to buy, the checkout names the jobs their stars came from, then what is being bought, what it costs and what is kept. Both parents say yes to a purchase, and the whole basket is answered at once.

Why it works this way

Questly's rules come from pediatric guidance and research on children, praise and rewards. Each one is linked to its source below.

A tap asks. A parent pays.

The American Academy of Pediatrics calls a parent's attention "the most powerful tool for effective discipline" and advises noticing good behavior and pointing it out.1 The CDC says specific praise works best "because your child knows exactly what you like."2 So in Questly the reward always comes from a person who saw the job, never from the app.

Both parents, one answer

The American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry advises that "parents should begin by talking to each other" about rules, and calls consistency and predictability "the cornerstones of discipline."3 Researchers who study co-parenting treat agreement between parents as one of its core parts.4 Both parents get the same screen and the same choices, and a purchase needs both of you.

Done is agreed in advance

Each job carries a standard you write: what done looks like. The same guidance from AACAP says parents and child "should decide together what the rewards and consequences will be,"3 and an author of the AAP's discipline policy advises setting rules in advance and being consistent in following through with them.8

Three answers, never silence

Every request gets yes, "not yet" with a reason, or no. The AAP advises "praising success and good tries."1 In a series of experiments, children praised for their effort kept trying and did better after a setback than children praised for being smart,5 and Carol Dweck, who led that work, describes a grade of "not yet" as telling a child they are on a learning curve, not that they failed.6

Right away, on both phones

The CDC notes that rewards that happen right after a behavior are best.2 The moment your child asks, both your phones buzz, and the stars can land while the job is still fresh.

No fines, no deductions

The AAP points parents to healthy forms of discipline such as setting limits, redirecting and setting expectations,7 and an author of that policy says "it's best to begin with the premise of rewarding positive behavior."8 Stars in Questly only go up. The only way down is a parent undoing their own mistaken approval.

Kindness is never on the price list

In a classic study, preschoolers who drew in order to get a promised award later chose drawing less often than children who received the same award as a surprise or no award at all.9 A review of 128 experiments found that unexpected rewards did not have that effect.10 So kindness and initiative cannot be asked for in Questly. A parent can only grant them, unprompted, as a one-star moment.

No streaks, no lures, no ads

A study of the apps used by 160 children aged three to five found manipulative design, such as fake time pressure and rewards for coming back, in most of them.11 Questly has no streaks, no countdowns, no ads and nothing to buy inside it. It is only open as long as a job takes.

Stars that buy real things

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau found that the skills behind good money habits, like planning ahead and self-control, start forming between ages three and five, with money habits taking shape between six and twelve.12 A saving goal, a basket that shows what is left, and a checkout that names where the stars came from give those skills somewhere to practise.5

These sources shaped how Questly works. They are about children and parenting in general. None of them studied Questly, and Questly is not a treatment, therapy or medical device.

Questions

What devices does it run on?

Any recent iPhone, iPad, Android phone or tablet, or computer with an up to date web browser. Questly is installed to the home screen like an app. On iPhone and iPad, notifications need iOS or iPadOS 16.4 or later, and Questly added to the Home Screen.

How many children can use it?

The launch price covers up to two children and two parents, each with their own sign in. If your family is bigger, tell us when you get in touch and we will tell you honestly what it takes.

What do you need from us?

Your names, what your children call you, the chores and rewards you want to start with, and photos or a description of each person so we can draw the characters. A parent also gives us written consent before we set up your children's sign ins. Everything else we set up for you.

Does it use real money?

No. Stars are your family's own currency, and the rewards are things you give. You can set what a dollar is worth in stars so a reward's price means something, but Questly never holds or moves money.

What happens after two years?

Questly keeps working. Support, fixes and updates end unless you choose to renew them, and we will remind you before your support period ends. The details are in the Questly terms.

What is a character update?

Once during your two years, we redraw the characters for the people already in your Questly, for example when your children have grown or changed their look. It is free.

Who can see our family's information?

Only your family, and Brightmade when we are helping you. Each family's Questly has its own separate database. We never sell it or use it for ads. See the Questly privacy policy.

What if it is not right for us?

Tell us within 14 days of your Questly being delivered and we will refund you in full. The Questly terms have the details.
