Questly

Two parents asked us for a chores and rewards app where they would both answer every job together, so their child would never have to ask the same question twice.

Private app

Their child was six, and a six year old cannot read a dashboard. So the request was for a place instead of a page.

We built them a floating island under a painted sky. Their child opens the app and stands on it, with the stars they have earned glowing overhead and only the jobs that belong to right now. Both parents get the same screen, the same answers and the same record, just as they asked. It is a private web app installed to the family's own devices.

First thing in the morning

The whole day, in one place

A child opens it and is standing on an island under a morning sky. Above are the stars saved so far and the thing they are being saved for. Below are the only jobs that belong to right now, never more than about five, each one a picture before it is a word. There is nothing to read and nowhere else to go.

Saying it is done

The tap that asks, not pays

A child taps the job that is finished. The stars do not move. The job turns and says it is with both parents, the character stands there thinking, and two phones buzz once. Nothing a child does in this app credits a point on its own, and that is visible on the screen rather than explained.

On a parent's phone

Three answers, never silence

A parent sees what is waiting and how long it has been there, with the standard for that job on the card: what done looks like, agreed in advance. Approve, and the stars land. Send it back with one of five short reasons and it returns to the list today. Or close it for the day, with a reason. All three go in the record.

What the stars are for

A shop to walk around

Real rewards, with prices a parent set. A child fills a basket and watches the total move against what is left, so what is affordable gets found out by trying. Nothing out of reach is hidden or locked. It offers to become the thing being saved for instead, which is a decision a child makes rather than one that happens to them.

At the checkout

Where the stars came from

This is the moment the app is built around. Before the asking, it names the jobs the stars in hand came from, then what is being bought, what it costs and what is kept. Both parents have to say yes, and the whole basket is paid for at once or not at all.

Wanting something else

Draw it instead

There is no order form and nothing to type. A child draws what they want on a sheet of paper, in three pens and ten colours, and the drawing is what their parents see. The two of them talk about it away from the app, then give it a name and a price, and it appears in the shop. Every drawing ever made stays hung up for them.

  • Nothing in it can be deleted
