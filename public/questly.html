Questly

A chores and rewards app where both parents answer every job, so a child never has to ask the same question twice.

Private app

A six year old cannot read a dashboard. Questly is a place instead of a page.

A child opens it and stands on a floating island under a painted sky, with their stars glowing overhead and only the jobs that belong to right now. Both parents get the same screen, the same answers and the same record. It is a private web app installed to the family's own devices.

What the stars are for

A shop to walk around

Real rewards, with prices a parent set. A child fills a basket and watches the total move against what is left, so what is affordable gets found out by trying. Nothing out of reach is hidden or locked. It offers to become the thing being saved for instead, which is a decision a child makes rather than one that happens to them.

Wanting something else

Draw it instead

There is no order form and nothing to type. A child draws what they want on a sheet of paper, in three pens and ten colours, and the drawing is what their parents see. The two of them talk about it away from the app, then give it a name and a price, and it appears in the shop. Every drawing ever made stays hung up for them.

The rest of the day

The tap that asks, not pays. A child taps the job that is finished and the stars do not move. The job goes to both parents and waits, and two phones buzz once. The buzz carries a count and nothing else, so neither parent can answer it from a lock screen without looking at the job first.

Three answers, never silence. A parent sees what is waiting and how long it has been there, with the standard for that job on the card: what done looks like, agreed in advance. Approve, and the stars land. Send it back with one of five short reasons and it returns to the list the same day. Or close it for the day, with a reason. All three go in the record.

Where the stars came from. At the checkout the app names the jobs the stars in hand came from, then what is being bought, what it costs and what is kept. Both parents have to say yes, and the whole basket is paid for at once or not at all.

A film in two voices. The app opens with a one minute film in both parents' own voices. It is theirs, so it stays in the family.
